A weekday, start to finish

Good Morning Dashboard

My partner rides two Regional Rail lines to work: one into Center City, and a different one back out. This is what the house tells them along the way: a glance at the kitchen display in the morning, and a push on the phone before heading home.

    Why I built it

    A trip the SEPTA app can't plan

    Every Regional Rail line runs through Center City, so you can ride any line in and a different line back out. The SEPTA app plans one line at a time. My partner's commute needs two, both ways.

    More

    It's a small FastAPI service on the same Raspberry Pi as the theater. Home Assistant asks it for the latest every 30 seconds and shows the answer on a wall tablet in the kitchen. A scheduler inside the service sends the afternoon pushes through ntfy.

    Chapter 1 of 5

    Two lines, one trip

    It plans each leg on its own, then pairs them with the earliest connection you can still make once live delays are counted. You choose where to change trains: try it under the screens.

    Try it

    Now it gets in 3 minutes after the connection is due out. That can still work, so the card warns instead of giving up.

    Seven minutes too late. It moves to the next connection and shows the new arrival time.

    More

    If SEPTA lists a direct train, it uses that first. The transfer station is a setting, so it works for any two lines that meet in Center City. If no connection works there, it tries the other Center City stations and says where to change instead. The display, the push and the follow-ups all read the same plan, so they never disagree.     Chapter 2 of 5

    Leave by, not departs

    The display shows when to walk out the door. Leave-by counts the walk to the station, and it moves when the train does.

    Try it

    Five more minutes at home. It still makes the same connection, so the arrival time doesn't change.

    Within three minutes of leave-by, the card turns red and says so.

    More

    The train's live position comes from SEPTA's TrainView feed, so a train sitting at the same stop for too long shows up as stalled before SEPTA's delay number catches up. The card only shows on weekdays during commute hours; the rest of the time it's the weather.

    Chapter 3 of 5

    The ride home comes to you

    Nobody's in the kitchen at the end of the workday, so the afternoon answer goes to the phone: one push at 4:00 with the train home.

    Try it

    The train to catch, when it gets home, the next trains that still connect, and rain on the way, before anyone has to go looking.

    More

    The evening trip is the morning one in reverse, on the other line first. Without GPS it can't know which train my partner actually makes it to, so the push lists up to two later trains too, but only ones whose connection clearly works. See the code

    Chapter 4 of 5

    Only buzz when it's worse

    After the 4:00 push, it checks again every five minutes, even after my partner boards, until the connecting train leaves. It only pushes again if the news got worse.

    Try it

    Late is worse than "looks good", so it buzzes.

    Same news, two minutes more: the display updates, and the phone stays quiet.

    Twenty minutes late misses the connection. That's worse, so it buzzes with the next connection and the new arrival time.

    More

    Every kind of push has a rank, from "looks good" up to cancelled and service alerts. A follow-up goes out when the rank goes up, or when the same kind of delay grows by another 10 minutes. Once the first train leaves, SEPTA's trip planner stops listing it, so the follow-ups read its live delay from SEPTA's train positions instead. A late first train that still makes its connection doesn't buzz at all. See the code

    Chapter 5 of 5

    Silence is never "all good"

    A push that never arrives looks exactly like a push saying everything's fine. So when something breaks, it still says something.

    Try it

    It uses the last good answer from a few minutes ago, and says so.

    Even a crash ends in a push: "Check SEPTA manually."

    More

    SEPTA, the live train positions, the service alerts and the weather each keep their last good answer. If a check fails, the old answer is used and flagged, on the display and in the push. And SEPTA's delay text is free-form ("On time", "5 min", "Suspended"), so text it can't read is never treated as on time. The full story

    Summary

    What I learned

    Connections
    Pairing two lines is the hard part: the right connection depends on the schedule, the station and live delays.
    Real APIs
    SEPTA's has quirks you only find by using it: the result count is req3, and the parameter that looks right, top, is ignored.
    Glanceable
    One line that answers "when do I leave?" beats a screen of train times.
    Quiet
    A push that buzzes for every minute gets ignored, so each one has to be worth it.
    Polite
    Answers under a minute old are reused, and nothing calls SEPTA outside commute hours: about 1,500 calls a weekday instead of 4,300.
    Fail loud
    When it can't tell, it says so instead of staying quiet.
    Tests
    Over 140 tests run on every commit, including one for each bug that made it to a real commute.

    Most mornings nobody thinks about it, and that's the point. The code is on GitHub.
